        "<div align=\"center\">\n",
        "\n",
        "# Why Were Transformers Invented?\n",
        "### The real evolution from ANN, CNN, RNN, LSTM and GRU to Transformers\n",
        "\n",
        "**Deep Learning · NLP · Architecture Evolution**\n",
        "\n",
        "</div>\n",
        "\n",
        "---\n",
        "\n",
        "> **Core idea:** Researchers did not invent Transformers because earlier neural networks were useless. They introduced them because sequence models—especially recurrent models—had limitations that became increasingly important for large-scale language processing and training.\n",
        "\n",
        "## 1. The journey before Transformers\n",
        "\n",
        "| Architecture | What it was designed to do | Important limitation |\n",
        "|---|---|---|\n",
        "| **ANN** | Learn patterns through interconnected neurons. | Does not inherently model sequential relationships. |\n",
        "| **CNN** | Learn spatial patterns, especially in images. | Ordinary CNNs do not naturally provide unrestricted direct interactions between distant sequence positions. |\n",
        "| **RNN** | Process sequences while carrying information from earlier steps. | Sequential computation limits parallelization; preserving long-range information can be difficult. |\n",
        "| **LSTM** | Improve recurrent memory using gates and a cell state. | Still processes sequences recurrently; long-range dependencies can remain challenging. |\n",
        "| **GRU** | Offer a simpler gated recurrent alternative to LSTM. | Still retains sequential computation. |\n",
        "| **Transformer** | Learn relationships between sequence elements using attention. | Standard full self-attention can be memory- and computation-intensive for long sequences. |\n",
        "\n",
        "<div style=\"border-left: 5px solid #16a34a; padding: 10px 14px; background: #f0fdf4;\">\n",
        "\n",
        "**Green highlight — Important:** These architectures were not simply replacing one another. They address different kinds of problems, and many remain useful today. CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs and GRUs have not become universally obsolete.\n",
        "\n",
        "</div>\n",
        "\n",
        "## 2. The language problem that motivated better sequence models\n",
        "\n",
        "Imagine translating:\n",
        "\n",
        "> The animal didn't cross the street because **it** was too tired.\n",
        "\n",
        "To translate the sentence correctly, a model needs to infer that *it* likely refers to the animal.\n",
        "\n",
        "Now consider:\n",
        "\n",
        "> The animal didn't cross the street because **it** was too wide.\n",
        "\n",
        "Here, *it* refers to the street.\n",
        "\n",
        "A language model must learn relationships between words. The meaning of a word can depend on other words elsewhere in the sentence. This is one form of a **long-range dependency**.\n",
        "\n",
        "### How an RNN processes a sentence\n",
        "\n",
        "```text\n",
        "The → animal → didn't → cross → the → street → because → it → was → too → wide\n",
        "```\n",
        "\n",
        "At each time step, an RNN updates its hidden state:\n",
        "\n",
        "\\[\n",
        "h_t = f(x_t, h_{t-1})\n",
        "\\]\n",
        "\n",
        "Where:\n",
        "\n",
        "- \\(x_t\\): representation of the current token.\n",
        "- \\(h_{t-1}\\): hidden state from the previous time step.\n",
        "- \\(h_t\\): updated hidden state.\n",
        "\n",
        "The hidden state carries information forward through the sequence.\n",
        "\n",
        "<div style=\"border-left: 5px solid #dc2626; padding: 10px 14px; background: #fef2f2;\">\n",
        "\n",
        "**Red highlight — Limitation:** Information from earlier tokens must pass through recurrent steps. As sequences become long, learning and preserving distant relationships can be difficult.\n",
        "\n",
        "</div>\n",
        "\n",
        "## 3. Why LSTM and GRU were not the final answer\n",
        "\n",
        "LSTM and GRU improved recurrent networks by using gates to regulate information flow.\n",
        "\n",
        "- **LSTM:** Uses input, forget and output gates, along with a cell state.\n",
        "- **GRU:** Uses update and reset gates in a more compact recurrent design.\n",
        "\n",
        "These improvements helped with learning longer-term dependencies. But both architectures still rely on recurrence.\n",
        "\n",
        "```text\n",
        "RNN / LSTM / GRU:\n",
        "\n",
        "Time step 1 → Time step 2 → Time step 3 → Time step 4\n",
        "```\n",
        "\n",
        "The next recurrent state depends on the previous one. That limits parallel computation across time steps during training.\n",
        "\n",
        "<div style=\"border-left: 5px solid #dc2626; padding: 10px 14px; background: #fef2f2;\">\n",
        "\n",
        "**Red highlight — Bottleneck:** GPUs are good at parallel computation, but the dependency between recurrent steps restricts how much of a sequence can be processed simultaneously.\n",
        "\n",
        "</div>\n",
        "\n",
        "Researchers wanted a way to model relationships across tokens without requiring the same recurrent chain.\n",
        "\n",
        "## 4. Attention: a crucial step toward Transformers\n",
        "\n",
        "**Attention existed before the Transformer.** Earlier sequence-to-sequence systems used attention to help a decoder focus on relevant parts of an input, rather than relying only on a single compressed representation.\n",
        "\n",
        "For example, while generating a translated word, a model could assign more attention to the source words relevant to that word.\n",
        "\n",
        "Researchers then explored a larger idea:\n",
        "\n",
        "> What if attention became the central mechanism of the architecture, rather than an additional component attached to a recurrent network?\n",
        "\n",
        "That idea helped lead to the Transformer.\n",
        "\n",
        "## 5. 2017 — The Transformer arrives\n",
        "\n",
        "The paper **“Attention Is All You Need”** was published in 2017. Its eight authors were Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones, Aidan N. Gomez, Łukasz Kaiser and Illia Polosukhin.\n",
        "\n",
        "The paper introduced an attention-based encoder–decoder architecture for sequence transduction, including machine translation. Its main sequence-processing structure dispensed with recurrence and convolution.\n",
        "\n",
        "### The central change\n",
        "\n",
        "**RNN-style processing:**\n",
        "\n",
        "```text\n",
        "Token 1 → Token 2 → Token 3 → Token 4 → Token 5\n",
        "```\n",
        "\n",
        "**Self-attention (simplified illustration):**\n",
        "\n",
        "```text\n",
        "Token 1 ↔ Token 2 ↔ Token 3 ↔ Token 4 ↔ Token 5\n",
        "```\n",
        "\n",
        "In a standard full self-attention layer, each token can incorporate information from other permitted positions. The model learns which relationships are useful during training.\n",
        "\n",
        "For example, the representation of **“it”** can attend to **“street”** in the sentence about the street being too wide.\n",
        "\n",
        "<div style=\"border-left: 5px solid #16a34a; padding: 10px 14px; background: #f0fdf4;\">\n",
        "\n",
        "**Green highlight — Breakthrough:** Self-attention provides direct connections between sequence positions, while the Transformer can process positions in parallel within a layer.\n",
        "\n",
        "</div>\n",
        "\n",
        "## 6. Why self-attention mattered\n",
        "\n",
        "| Property | RNN | Transformer with self-attention |\n",
        "|---|---|---|\n",
        "| Sequence computation | Recurrent dependency between time steps | Token positions can be processed in parallel within a layer |\n",
        "| Distant relationships | Information passes through recurrent steps | Direct attention connections can link distant positions |\n",
        "| Long-range dependencies | May be difficult to learn | Shorter information paths can help |\n",
        "| Long-sequence cost | Depends on recurrent computation and model design | Standard full self-attention has quadratic sequence-length cost |\n",
        "\n",
        "**Important qualification:** Transformers are not automatically faster or cheaper for every task. Standard full self-attention has computation and memory costs that grow approximately quadratically with sequence length. RNNs can still be useful for some streaming or sequential applications.\n",
        "\n",
        "## 7. What happened after the Transformer?\n",
        "\n",
        "The original Transformer targeted sequence-to-sequence tasks. Researchers subsequently adapted Transformer components and training objectives to many other tasks.\n",
        "\n",
        "| Year | Development | Significance |\n",
        "|---|---|---|\n",
        "| **2017** | Original Transformer | Introduced an attention-centered sequence-transduction architecture. |\n",
        "| **2018** | BERT | Demonstrated the effectiveness of Transformer encoders for language representations. |\n",
        "| **2018 onward** | GPT models | Developed decoder-only Transformer language models for autoregressive text generation. |\n",
        "| **2020** | Vision Transformer (ViT) | Applied Transformer processing to image patches for image recognition. |\n",
        "| **2020s** | Large-scale generative AI systems | Transformer-based models became central to many widely used language and multimodal systems. |\n",
        "\n",
        "These models do not all share an identical architecture or training procedure. They adapt Transformer designs to different goals.\n",
        "\n",
        "## 8. Why Transformers became important for Generative AI\n",
        "\n",
        "A language model learns patterns in text, including grammar, context and relationships between tokens. Transformer architectures provide mechanisms for learning these relationships, and their parallelizable training structure makes them suitable for large-scale training.\n",
        "\n",
        "### Autoregressive generation in a decoder-only model\n",
        "\n",
        "```text\n",
        "Context: Artificial intelligence is\n",
        "                         ↓\n",
        "                 Predict next token\n",
        "                         ↓\n",
        "                       changing\n",
        "                         ↓\n",
        "          Append token and predict again\n",
        "```\n",
        "\n",
        "During training, an autoregressive model learns to predict the next token from preceding context. At generation time, it repeatedly predicts and appends tokens.\n",
        "\n",

        "## 10. The evolution in one diagram\n",
        "\n",
        "```text\n",

        " │  Learn patterns from data\n",

        " ▼\n",
        "RNN\n",

        " ▼\n",
        "LSTM / GRU\n",
        " │  Improve information flow and recurrent memory\n",
        " ▼\n",
        "Attention\n",
        " │  Focus on relevant parts of the input\n",
        " ▼\n",
        "Transformer (2017)\n",

        " ▼\n",
